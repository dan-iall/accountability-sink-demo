### 1.2 Random Assignment & SUTVA

Participants are allocated to $D_i \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ via stratified block randomization across recruitment waves. Randomization ensures joint independence of potential outcomes and potential mediators from treatment assignment:

$$\{Y_i(k), \mathbf{M}_i(k)\}_{k=1}^{4} \perp D_i \quad \text{(within randomization strata)}$$

Isolated browser sessions and scripted subordinate agents (*Alex, Sara, Lukas*) eliminate peer spillover, worker-quality heterogeneity, and cross-subject interference, satisfying the Stable Unit Treatment Value Assumption (SUTVA; Imbens & Rubin, 2015). Under random assignment and SUTVA, the Intention-to-Treat Average Treatment Effect for arm $k \in \{2,3,4\}$ relative to Control ($k=1$) is non-parametrically identified as:

$$\text{ATE}_{k,1} = \mathbb{E}\!\left[Y_i^{\text{obs}} \mid D_i = k\right] - \mathbb{E}\!\left[Y_i^{\text{obs}} \mid D_i = 1\right]$$

### 1.3 Tripartite Protocol for Shift 2 Directive ($W_{\text{S2},i}$)

Arms 1, 2, and 4 share an identical user interface and task flow through the end of Shift 2, whereas Arm 3 administers its experiential floor rotation *before* Shift 1 (Screen 1B). Consequently, the Shift 2 escalation choice $W_{\text{S2},i} = \mathbf{1}(\text{s2b\_shift2\_directive}_i = \text{"B\_SLOW\_FLOOR"})$ is **post-treatment** in Arm 3 ($D_3 \to W_{\text{S2},i}$) but **pre-treatment** within the $\{1, 2, 4\}$ arms. To preserve causal identification while exploiting its behavioral signal, $W_{\text{S2},i}$ is governed by a strict **Tripartite Protocol**:

1. **Omitted from All 4-Arm ATE Estimators:** $W_{\text{S2},i}$ is strictly excluded as a regressor in full-sample 4-arm specifications. Conditioning on $W_{\text{S2},i}$ across all four arms would introduce both **overcontrol bias** (blocking the causal pathway $D_3 \to W_{\text{S2},i} \to Y_i$) and **collider bias** (opening a spurious path $D_3 \to W_{\text{S2},i} \leftarrow U_i \to Y_i$ via unobserved managerial conscientiousness $U_i$; Pearl, 2009).
2. **Pooled First-Stage Behavioral Outcome (Arm 3 vs. $\{1,2,4\}$):** Because Arms 1, 2, and 4 are operationally indistinguishable at Shift 2, we test whether firsthand operator friction increases proactive quota relief by estimating the unadjusted difference-in-means comparing Arm 3 ($n=60$) against pooled $\{1,2,4\}$ ($n=180$):
   $$W_{\text{S2},i} = \alpha_{\text{S2}} + \tau_{\text{S2}} D_{3i} + \nu_i \quad \text{(HC3 SEs; conservative MDE} = 20.9\text{ pp at } p_0 = 0.50\text{, } R^2 = 0\text{)}$$
3. **Balance Check & Demeaned Lin (2013) Covariate Within $\{1,2,4\}$:** Restricting the sample to $D_i \in \{1, 2, 4\}$, $W_{\text{S2},i}$ is strictly pre-treatment relative to the Screen 3 forensic Box D manipulation. We first verify arm balance across $\{1, 2, 4\}$ (expecting joint $p > 0.20$) and then include demeaned $\widetilde{W}_{\text{S2},i} = W_{\text{S2},i} - \bar{W}_{\text{S2},\{1,2,4\}}$ interacted with treatment indicators ($D_{2i} \times \widetilde{W}_{\text{S2},i}$ and $D_{4i} \times \widetilde{W}_{\text{S2},i}$) following **Lin (2013)** to improve finite-sample precision without introducing bias into the $\text{ATE}_{2,1}$ and $\text{ATE}_{4,1}$ estimates.

## 2. Primary Confirmatory Specifications

### 2.1 Model 1 — Primary Binary Sanctioning (`Task2_Penalize_Sara_Binary`)

Our primary outcome $Y_{\text{disc},i} \in \{0,1\}$ records whether the manager elects to penalize the operator (`Option 1`: restoring the `+300 pt` hold to preserve the €4.00 bonus) rather than absorb institutional liability (`Option 2`). We estimate both a **Logit model reporting Average Marginal Effects ($\text{AME}_k$)** and an **OLS Linear Probability Model (LPM) with MacKinnon & White (1985) HC3** heteroskedasticity-consistent standard errors (omitting $W_{\text{S2},i}$ per Section 1.3):

$$\Pr\!\left(Y_{\text{disc},i} = 1 \mid \mathbf{D}_i, \mathbf{X}_i\right) = \Lambda\!\left(\beta_0 + \beta_2 D_{2i} + \beta_3 D_{3i} + \beta_4 D_{4i} + \boldsymbol{\gamma}'\mathbf{X}_i\right)$$

$$Y_{\text{disc},i} = \alpha_0 + \tau_2 D_{2i} + \tau_3 D_{3i} + \tau_4 D_{4i} + \boldsymbol{\theta}'\mathbf{X}_i + u_i \quad \text{(HC3 SEs)}$$

Unadjusted difference-in-means estimates ($\mathbf{X}_i$ omitted) are reported alongside covariate-adjusted specifications as the design-based baseline. Pre-registered primary hypotheses (evaluated two-tailed at $\alpha = 0.05$ with Romano–Wolf FWER adjustment across the primary family):

- **$H_1$ (Baseline Scapegoating):** Tests whether baseline sanctioning under opacity exceeds majority incidence, $\Pr(Y_{\text{disc},i}=1 \mid D_i=1) > 0.50$.
- **$H_2$ (STAMP Informational Telemetry):** Tests whether surfacing the $-23.5\text{s}$ ergonomic deficit reduces operator sanctioning relative to Control ($\text{AME}_2 < 0$).
- **$H_3$ (Experiential Friction vs. Informational Telemetry):** Tests whether firsthand pre-shift operator experience reduces sanctioning relative to Control and exceeds purely analytical telemetry ($\text{AME}_3 < \text{AME}_2 < 0$).
- **$H_4$ (Statutory Legal Notice):** Tests whether surfacing EU AI Act deployer-duty provisions reduces operator sanctioning relative to Control ($\text{AME}_4 < 0$). Because Arm 4 also surfaces the $-23.5\text{s}$ ergonomic deficit, the planned active-arm contrast **`Arm 4 vs. Arm 2`** ($\text{AME}_4 - \text{AME}_2$) isolates the incremental expressive legal effect over pure ergonomic telemetry.
- **$H_5$ (Optional Attention Mechanism — AOI / Eye-Tracking Extension):** In browser-telemetry and optional Tobii Pro eye-tracking subsamples, tests whether higher proportional diagnostic dwell on forensic Box D versus the categorical AI badge predicts lower sanctioning ($\beta_{\text{gaze}} < 0$).

### 2.2 Model 2 — Within-Subject Incentive Distortion (Stacked Binary-to-Binary + Continuous Robustness)

To test whether financial stakes distort managerial blame downward relative to unincentivized private beliefs, we stack each participant's **Stage I unincentivized operator-blame indicator** ($t=1$, $\text{MoneyTask}_1 = 0$) and **Stage II payoff-contingent sanctioning decision** ($t=2$, $\text{MoneyTask}_2 = 1$) into a $2N$-observation within-subject panel ($t \in \{1, 2\}$):

$$Y_{i,t} = \alpha + \sum_{k=2}^{4} \beta_k D_{ki} + \delta\,\text{MoneyTask}_t + \sum_{k=2}^{4} \gamma_k \left(D_{ki} \times \text{MoneyTask}_t\right) + \varepsilon_{i,t}$$

- **Primary Specification (Binary-to-Binary):** Sets $Y_{i,1} = \mathbf{1}(\text{Task1B\_Root\_Cause\_Code}_i = \text{"H1"})$ and $Y_{i,2} = Y_{\text{disc},i}$ (`Task2_Penalize_Sara_Binary`), placing both stages on an identical $\{0,1\}$ probability scale so $\delta$ and $\gamma_k$ are directly interpretable in percentage points. Standard errors are computed using **subject-clustered CR3 (leave-one-cluster-out jackknife)** variance estimation clustered at participant ID `pid` (MacKinnon, Nielsen, & Webb, 2023), with $t$-distribution inference on $G - 1 = N - 1$ degrees of freedom.
  - **$\delta$ (`MoneyTask`):** Captures the baseline attribution-to-sanction shift in Control once the €4.00 bonus is at stake (hypothesized $\delta > 0$). Because Stage I is elicited prior to Stage II, psychological consistency pressure (Falk & Zimmermann, 2013) pulls Stage II choices toward Stage I attributions, making $\hat{\delta}$ a **conservative lower bound** on pure financial distortion—while noting that $\delta$ also reflects any baseline construct difference between Stage I 3-way root-cause attribution (`H-1`) and Stage II binary firing.
  - **$\gamma_k$ (`D_k × MoneyTask`):** Identifies randomized treatment attenuation of the attribution-to-sanction gap (hypothesized $\gamma_k < 0$). Any arm-invariant construct difference between Stage I and Stage II cancels out in $\gamma_k$; moreover, in this balanced 2-period panel ($t \in \{1,2\}$), $\hat{\delta}$ and $\hat{\gamma}_k$ are algebraically identical to a 1-row-per-manager first-difference ($\Delta Y_i = Y_{i,2} - Y_{i,1}$) or subject fixed-effects ($\alpha_i$) regression while keeping Stage I baseline arm effects ($\beta_k$) visible in the same specification.
- **Secondary Robustness Check (Continuous-to-Binary):** Re-estimates the exact same $2N$ stacked equation with subject-clustered CR3 SEs replacing $Y_{i,1}$ with the continuous bounded Stage I operator blame share $S_{\text{Sara},i} = R_{\text{Sara},i} / (R_{\text{Sara},i} + R_{\text{AI},i} + R_{\text{SLA},i}) \in [0,1]$ (`sara_share_frac`), verifying directional consistency across discrete and continuous Stage I operationalizations. Both specifications are implemented and verified in `analysis/power_simulation.py`.

## 3. Secondary & Exploratory Estimator Suite

To preserve confirmatory parsimony in the executive proposal while documenting full mechanism tests prior to data collection, the secondary and exploratory estimators below complement Models 1–2.

### 3.1 Multivariate Attribution & Blame Reallocation (Stacked OLS / SUR)

Because Stage I elicits three independent `0–10` responsibility sliders ($R_{\text{Sara},i}, R_{\text{AI},i}, R_{\text{SLA},i}$) from the same respondent, error terms are correlated across attribution targets. We estimate the three equations jointly via **Stacked OLS / Seemingly Unrelated Regressions (SUR)** with an unrestricted $3 \times 3$ cross-equation error covariance matrix $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_\varepsilon$ (Wooldridge, 2010):

$$R_{m,i} = \alpha_m + \sum_{k=2}^{4} \delta_{k,m} D_{ki} + \boldsymbol{\psi}_m'\mathbf{X}_i + \varepsilon_{m,i}, \qquad m \in \{\text{Sara}, \text{AI}, \text{SLA}\}$$

With identical regressors across equations, point estimates coincide with equation-by-equation OLS while the joint covariance matrix enables cross-equation Wald tests of **Blame Reallocation**—specifically testing whether treatments simultaneously reduce frontline blame and increase structural-quota attribution:

$$H_A^{\text{realloc}}:\quad \delta_{k,\text{Sara}} < 0 \;\land\; \delta_{k,\text{SLA}} > 0 \qquad (k \in \{2,3,4\})$$

### 3.2 Bounded Operator Blame Share (Papke & Wooldridge, 1996 Fractional Logit)

The normalized Stage I operator blame share $S_{\text{Sara},i} = R_{\text{Sara},i} / \sum_{m} R_{m,i} \in [0, 1]$ (`sara_share_frac`) is bounded on the unit interval with positive mass near $0$ and $1$, where linear regression can yield out-of-bounds predictions and log-odds transformations break down at boundaries. Following **Papke & Wooldridge (1996)**, we estimate a **Fractional Logit** quasi-maximum likelihood model (Bernoulli log-likelihood with logit link and HC3 sandwich standard errors):

$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[S_{\text{Sara},i} \mid \mathbf{D}_i, \mathbf{X}_i\right] = \Lambda\!\left(\phi_0 + \phi_2 D_{2i} + \phi_3 D_{3i} + \phi_4 D_{4i} + \boldsymbol{\eta}'\mathbf{X}_i\right)$$

Both log-odds coefficients and Average Partial Effects (APEs in percentage points of blame share) are reported.

### 3.3 Model 3 — Categorical Root-Cause Classification (Multinomial Logit)

For the forced-choice Stage I primary root-cause classification `Task1B_Root_Cause_Code` $\in \{\text{H-1: Operator}, \text{A-2: AI Copilot}, \text{M-3: SLA Constraint}\}$ (exported as `"H1"`, `"A2"`, `"M3"`), we fit a **Multinomial Logit** with operator negligence (`H-1`) as the base category:

$$\ln \frac{\Pr(\text{RootCause}_i = c \mid \mathbf{D}_i, \mathbf{X}_i)}{\Pr(\text{RootCause}_i = \text{H-1} \mid \mathbf{D}_i, \mathbf{X}_i)} = \kappa_{0,c} + \sum_{k=2}^{4} \kappa_{k,c} D_{ki} + \boldsymbol{\lambda}_c'\mathbf{X}_i, \qquad c \in \{\text{A-2}, \text{M-3}\}$$

We report **Relative Risk Ratios** $\text{RRR}_{k,\text{M-3}} = \exp(\kappa_{k,\text{M-3}})$, testing whether governance interventions shift primary causal diagnosis from individual negligence toward systemic time compression ($\text{RRR}_{k,\text{M-3}} > 1$), alongside Independence of Irrelevant Alternatives (IIA) diagnostics.

### 3.4 Model 4 — Exploratory Sequential Causal Mediation

To probe the hypothesized cognitive pathway $D_i \to M_{\text{gaze},i} \to M_{\text{control},i} \to Y_{\text{disc},i}$ (forensic diagnostic attention $\to$ perceived operator controllability $\to$ sanctioning), we apply the **Imai, Keele, & Tingley (2010)** and **Imai & Yamamoto (2013)** causally ordered multiple-mediator framework, decomposing total effects into Average Causal Mediation Effects (ACME) and Average Direct Effects (ADE).

- **Explicit Exploratory Qualification:** Because $M_{\text{gaze},i}$ and $M_{\text{control},i}$ are observed post-treatment rather than independently randomized, sequential ignorability is untestable and potentially violated by unobserved confounding between mediators and sanctioning. Model 4 is therefore strictly designated **exploratory and assumption-bound**.
- **Sensitivity Bounds & Design-Based Follow-Up:** All ACME estimates are reported alongside **$\tilde{\rho}$ sensitivity curves** identifying the mediator–outcome error correlation $\tilde{\rho}$ at which $\text{ACME}(\tilde{\rho}) = 0$. Causal confirmation of the attention channel is reserved for a **planned design-based mediator manipulation** (randomized encouragement / salience cueing of Box D orthogonally to treatment arms).

### 3.5 Model 5 — Dynamic Policy Reform & Crowding-Out (Bivariate Probit)

In Stage III (Shift 4), managers set the future SLA tier (`25s / 38s / 60s`), yielding the binary ergonomic compliance indicator $Y_{\text{reform},i} = \mathbf{1}(\text{Task4\_Shift4\_Sla\_Reform\_Sec}_i = 60.0\text{s} \ge 48.5\text{s})$. To test whether scapegoating the frontline operator substitutes for structural reform, we estimate a **Bivariate Probit** jointly modeling $(Y_{\text{disc},i}, Y_{\text{reform},i})$:

$$Y_{\text{disc},i}^{*} = \mathbf{z}_i'\boldsymbol{\pi}_1 + u_{\text{disc},i}, \qquad Y_{\text{reform},i}^{*} = \mathbf{z}_i'\boldsymbol{\pi}_2 + u_{\text{reform},i}, \qquad \begin{pmatrix} u_{\text{disc},i} \\ u_{\text{reform},i} \end{pmatrix} \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(\mathbf{0}, \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \rho_{\text{biv}} \\ \rho_{\text{biv}} & 1 \end{pmatrix}\right)$$

where $\mathbf{z}_i = (1, D_{2i}, D_{3i}, D_{4i}, \mathbf{X}_i')'$. A negative error correlation $\rho_{\text{biv}} = \text{Cov}(u_{\text{disc}}, u_{\text{reform}}) < 0$ (tested via likelihood-ratio test of $H_0: \rho_{\text{biv}} = 0$) is consistent with **reform crowding-out**: conditional on arm assignment, managers who penalize Sara are systematically less likely to relax the unsafe $25.0\text{s}$ SLA.

## 4. Pilot-Calibrated Power Architecture & Reproducible Simulation

### 4.1 Parameterized Sample-Size Governance

Because true baseline sanctioning ($p_1$), outcome variance ($\sigma$), and pre-treatment covariate explanatory power ($R_{\mathbf{X}}^2$) are unobserved prior to human data collection, final sample sizes are **parameterized rather than fixed in advance**. An initial calibration pilot ($n = 8\text{–}10/\text{arm}$, $N_{\text{pilot}} = 32\text{–}40$) verifies JSON-configurable task parameters (`tum_experiment_config.json`) against floor or ceiling saturation using **blinded Control-arm calibration**—estimating $(\hat{p}_1, \hat{\sigma}, \hat{R}_{\mathbf{X}}^2)$ without inspecting treatment contrasts, which preserves exact Type I error control ($\alpha = 0.05$). If participants lean strongly toward sanctioning under the €4.00 bonus hold ($p_1 \ge 0.75\text{–}0.80$), Bernoulli variance $p_1(1-p_1)$ shrinks and treatment separation $\Delta p$ widens, so smaller cohorts reach $80\%$ power (`Cohort 1`, $N_1 = 80\text{–}120$, $n = 20\text{–}30/\text{arm}$) than under a noisy $p_1 = 0.50$ split (`Cohort 2`, $N_2 = 240$, $n = 60/\text{arm}$).

## 5. Attrition Bounds, Falsification Battery & Planned Phase 2 Extensions

### 5.1 Differential Onboarding Attrition & Lee (2009) Trimming Bounds

Because Arm 3 requires completing two firsthand pre-shift operator trials on Screen 1B before entering Shift 1, Arm 3 faces a structured risk of higher onboarding dropout than Arms 1, 2, and 4. Assuming one-sided selection monotonicity—that additional onboarding friction never *induces* a participant who would have dropped out of Control to remain in Arm 3—we bound all treatment effects under differential attrition using **Lee (2009) trimming bounds**:

1. Compute arm-specific completion rates $r_k$ vs. $r_1$ and excess retention fraction $q_{k,1} = (r_1 - r_k)/r_1$ when $r_1 > r_k$ (symmetrically $q_{k,1} = (r_k - r_1)/r_k$ when $r_k > r_1$).
2. Trim the upper and lower $q_{k,1}$ tails of the higher-retention arm's outcome distribution to construct sharp worst-case and best-case bounds $[\widehat{\text{ATE}}_{k,1}^{\text{LB}}, \widehat{\text{ATE}}_{k,1}^{\text{UB}}]$ alongside Imbens–Manski 95% confidence intervals.
3. Pair trimming bounds with onboarding latency and drop-stage diagnostics (`s1b_executed`, `s1b_case2_dwell_sec`, `status`) to detect whether dropout concentrates on the `25.0s` timed trial.

### 5.2 Exact Randomization Inference & Stepdown FWER Control

To guard against asymptotic small-sample bias and multiplicity across arms:

- **Exact Fisher Randomization Inference (RI):** For every primary Model 1 and Model 2 treatment contrast, we compute exact two-sided $p$-values under the sharp null $H_0: Y_i(k) = Y_i(1)\;\forall i$ using **$10{,}000$ within-stratum permutations** of treatment assignment $D_i$.
- **Romano & Wolf (2005) Stepdown FWER Control:** Across the primary confirmatory hypothesis family ($H_1$–$H_4$ and primary stacked distortion contrasts), we compute studentized **Romano–Wolf stepdown adjusted $p$-values** ($10{,}000$ bootstrap resamples) that account for cross-hypothesis dependence while maintaining strict family-wise error rate control at $\alpha = 0.05$.

### 5.3 Pre-Treatment Placebo & Balance Battery

Before estimating treatment effects, we run a pre-registered falsification battery verifying that randomization holds prior to post-Shift-2 divergence (targeting joint and pairwise $p > 0.20$):

1. **Shift 1 Throughput Score Placebo:** Shift 1 (`+360 pts`) is mechanically identical across all arms; regressing Shift 1 completion telemetry and scores on $(D_{2i}, D_{3i}, D_{4i})$ must yield null differences ($p > 0.20$).
2. **Pre-Treatment Dwell & Attention Placebo:** Pre-treatment onboarding dwell times (Steps 1–5) and Screen 2A initial SLA preferences within $\{1, 2, 4\}$ must show null arm differences ($p > 0.20$).
3. **Joint Covariate Orthogonality & Normalized Differences:** A multinomial/F-test of treatment indicators on baseline demographics, management experience, and AI literacy—paired with standardized normalized differences ($|\bar{z}_k - \bar{z}_1| / \sigma_z < 0.10\text{ SD}$)—verifies randomization balance; any covariate exhibiting incidental imbalance ($p < 0.10$ or $|\text{NormDiff}| \ge 0.10$) is additionally verified under Lin (2013) interacted adjustment. Post-treatment diagnostic dwell and attention metrics are strictly retained as manipulation checks and exploratory mediators ($M_{\text{gaze},i}$), never used to subset Model 1 (preserving unbiased ITT identification).

### 5.4 Planned Phase 2 Extensions (Post-MVP Roadmap)

The current Phase 1 testbed isolates expressive statutory framing in Arm 4 under identical Stage II KPI displays during evaluation. Two dedicated extensions are strictly reserved for **planned Phase 2** (contingent on Phase 1 empirical findings and powered at Cohort 2, $N_2 = 240, n = 60/\text{arm}$):

1. **Arm 4a vs. Arm 4b Legal-Incentive Decoupling:** Splits Arm 4 into **`Arm 4a` (Expressive Notice Only, $\mathbb{E}[\text{fine}] = 0$)**—surfacing EU AI Act Arts. 14(4), 26(2), and 99(4)(e) with unchanged financial payoffs—versus **`Arm 4b` (Pecuniary Clawback)**, where penalizing Sara voids the `+300 pt` release and levies a `-50 pt` compliance penalty while absorbing liability awards a `+€1.00` integrity credit. Comparing `Arm 4a` vs. `Arm 4b` cleanly separates normative legal salience from direct financial deterrence.
2. **Paired-Worker Externality Check:** Replaces scripted subordinate payoffs with a real **paired-worker bonus pool** (Fehr & Schmidt, 1999), where a manager's `Option 1` sanction imposes an actual monetary deduction on a matched participant from a separate worker pool. This tests whether other-regarding fairness preferences attenuate or interact with institutional scapegoating relative to the scripted-agent baseline.
